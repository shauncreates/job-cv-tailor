# Vacancy-to-evidence mapping

Use this reference when comparing a candidate's material with a vacancy or when a numeric fit estimate would help.

## Read the vacancy as a set of claims

Extract only criteria that affect the role:

1. **Gates:** explicit eligibility, licence, clearance, location, hours, minimum age, or other conditions. Separate confirmed, unknown, and unmet; do not assume the candidate's status.
2. **Essential requirements:** required qualifications, experience, capabilities, or availability.
3. **Core duties:** the work the candidate would actually perform.
4. **Desirable criteria:** useful preferences, secondary duties, or context.

Record a short source phrase or faithful paraphrase and, for live listings, when and where it was checked. Do not treat an employer's boilerplate as a candidate fact.

## Evidence labels

- **Direct:** the candidate explicitly performed a closely matching task or holds the stated qualification. Cite the specific role, project, or credential.
- **Transferable / partial:** there is concrete adjacent evidence, but a meaningful difference remains. Name both the overlap and the limit.
- **Not evidenced:** the supplied materials do not show evidence. This says nothing definitive about ability.
- **Unclear / conflicting:** source details are ambiguous or inconsistent. Identify what needs checking.

For each important vacancy requirement, record: criterion, priority, evidence and source, label, confidence, and whether a follow-up is needed. Merge duplicate criteria, but preserve meaningful differences (for example, general customer service versus handling cash).

## Optional coverage estimate (0–100)

Use only when asked or decision-useful. This is a transparent coverage aid, not a validated hiring model, ATS score, or chance of interview.

1. Weight criteria by the vacancy: essential requirements 40 points, core duties 30, relevant experience 20, and desirable criteria 10. If the listing has no meaningful desirable criteria, redistribute that weight across the other groups and say how.
2. Within each group, divide points among distinct criteria according to importance; do not count repeated wording twice.
3. For each criterion, award full criterion points for specific direct evidence, about half for concrete but incomplete/transferable evidence, and zero for absent or unsupported evidence. Use judgement rather than false decimal precision.
4. Report the total as a rounded range when evidence is ambiguous. Show the group-level points and two or three strongest drivers. Keep unresolved eligibility gates separate from the score: an unknown gate stays unknown even if coverage is high.
5. If the material is too incomplete for a useful number, decline the score and explain what is missing.

Do not include CV design or keyword density in the evidence-coverage score. Report readability and evidence visibility separately. Never suggest the estimate reveals the employer's ATS logic.

## Recruiter skim review

Identify at most three high-impact risks. For each, state:

- what the recruiter may see or fail to find;
- which vacancy requirement makes it matter;
- whether the issue is missing evidence, weak placement/wording, or uncertainty;
- the smallest truthful remedy (rewrite, reorder, ask the candidate, or leave as a real gap).

Do not label a candidate unqualified based only on an omitted detail. Do not speculate about protected characteristics or infer motivation from a career gap, age, name, or personal circumstance.
