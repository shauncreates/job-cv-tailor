# Tailoring playbook

Use only the section relevant to the user's requested deliverable. Tailoring changes emphasis and clarity, not the candidate's history.

## CV or resume

1. Preserve the candidate's source document and extract a fact bank: roles, dates, duties, tools, scope, outcomes, qualifications, and stated skills.
2. Map evidence to the vacancy using `evidence-mapping.md`. Prioritise essential criteria and repeated core duties; do not mirror every phrase in the advert.
3. Draft a short, specific profile from supported evidence. Avoid generic self-ratings ("hard-working", "dynamic", "results-driven") unless the user requests a style that needs them and evidence follows.
4. Choose relevant skills and bullets. Strong bullets make clear what the candidate did, in what context, and what changed when that result is documented. If the source gives no result, do not invent one.
5. Preserve the exact job title and dates. Reorder bullets by relevance within a role, keeping roles reverse-chronological unless the user requests otherwise. Keep gaps and overlaps factual; do not conceal or explain them without evidence.
6. Check that the rewritten CV still represents the candidate's level of ownership accurately (assisted, performed, led, supervised, managed). Do not turn team outcomes into individual achievements.

## Cover letter or supporting statement

- Draft only when asked. Address the stated employer/role and selection criteria; do not invent a contact name or a personal reason for applying.
- Use a small number of concrete examples from the candidate's evidence. Explain relevance without repeating the CV paragraph for paragraph.
- Respect any word limit. If none is given, be concise and use a sensible structure for the requested market and role.
- Use placeholders sparingly for genuinely unknown facts, and call them out in the handoff so the candidate can complete them.

## Application-form answers

- Answer the exact question and keep within its stated limit. Use a specific example when requested; do not force STAR if another structure is clearer.
- Distinguish what the candidate did from what a team did. Do not claim availability, eligibility, salary expectations, or screening answers without evidence or confirmation.
- If the candidate's evidence is insufficient, draft a bounded answer or ask for the missing example. Never fill the gap with a plausible story.

## Before-and-after examples

These are fictional illustrations of the editing method, not reusable candidate claims.

**Weak:** "Excellent communicator with great teamwork skills."

**Evidence-led (when source supports it):** "Worked with three colleagues to prepare 40 online orders during each shift." The number and team context must appear in the source.

If the source only says "helped prepare online orders," retain that level of detail: "Helped prepare online orders for collection." Do not add a number, speed claim, customer outcome, or ownership.
